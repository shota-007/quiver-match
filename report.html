1. Motivation

Who has the problem. Most regular surfers own two to four boards, a "quiver": say a fish for small days, a shortboard for good days and a longboard for mush. Before every session, and especially before a trip, they face the same question: which board, for these waves? Travelers face a harder version. Airline board bags hold one or two boards, rental shops abroad are hit or miss, and the waves at a new spot are unknown. The right answer depends on three things at once: the surfer (weight, skill, fitness), the boards they own (length, volume, shape), and the real forecast for those days at that break.

How they handle it today. Surfers check a forecast app (Surfline), look up a brand volume calculator (Firewire, Channel Islands, Lost), ask friends or a shop, or now ask ChatGPT. None of these connects your quiver to this week's forecast at that spot.

What it costs them. Bringing the wrong board means a trip of missed waves or a rental fee on top of the board you carried. Buying the wrong board is a large purchase (a new board typically costs hundreds of dollars; careful estimate from common retail prices). The worst cost is safety: beginners on trips paddle out at heavy reefs (Uluwatu, Pipeline) or big beach breaks (Black's) because nobody connected the forecast to their level.

Real people. 2 real surfers shared their quivers and sessions for this project: A.L. (2 boards); M L (2 boards). Each one faces the same question before a session or trip: which of these boards, for these waves?

Industry input: the owner of Jack's Surfboards, a local surf shop and the father of one of the author's friends, suggested linking every recommendation to real shops near the break that rent or sell boards. That request became the "Where to rent / where to buy nearby" feature.

2. What is new

Board volume calculators exist and are a formula: height, weight and skill in, liters out. Quiver Match's twist is to combine the surfer's own quiver, the real forecast for the trip dates, the break type, and a safety check into one answer: what to ride each day, what to pack, what to rent, and where it's safe. The ChatGPT column is measured (section 6). The others describe published features.

Not a generic trip planner. Quiver Match doesn't plan itineraries, flights or hotels. It makes one equipment and safety decision, which board for which wave on which day and whether it's safe for this surfer, from real ocean data and the surfer's own boards. That is the part travel planners and chatbots can't do, because they have no forecast, no board math and no safety rules.

Sources: Firewire's calculator (Down the Line Surf, Kalon Surf); Boardline and Surfline (Rapture Camps). We did not test those products ourselves. Honest caveat: a plain ChatGPT answer costs about 21x fewer tokens than a Quiver Match plan; the extra tokens buy the real forecast, the per-day safety call and verified numbers.

Why it's organized this way

  • Code decides; agents judge and explain. Safety ratings, meters and dimensions are formulas, and the professor's rule applies: "conditions come from the forecast data, never the model guessing." The agents pick the design wave, choose between near-ties, and turn numbers into a plan a surfer can act on.
  • The Safety Agent only runs when needed. On a "go" trip it never runs, which saves tokens. On risky trips it is the only part allowed to suggest alternatives, and only from tool data.
  • A fixed pipeline with typed hand-offs. The order never changes, so a manager agent would add cost without value.
  • Model: gpt-6-luna via Portkey (the course model), Responses API with function tools and structured output, low reasoning effort (section 4 shows medium effort adds 60% output tokens with no quality gain).

The meter formula. r = board liters ÷ ideal liters for this surfer and day; lx = how much longer than ideal. Catching = 70 + 60(r−1) + 30·lx (−10 on tiny days if under-volumed). Control = 85 − 12·power·(excess volume + excess length), with flat-rocker boards losing hold above 3.5 ft and performance shapes gaining. On big, powerful days ("bigness" rises from 4.5 to 7.5 ft) undersized boards lose catching ease and control, and up to 15% extra size is not penalized. Turning = 95 − 0.9·(length − 64 in) − 0.5·(liters over 30). Fit weights them by the day (small/soft: catching 55%; big/powerful: control 55%; beginners weight catching more), and boards unsafe for the level are capped at 20. The big-day rule was added after the synthetic stress test (section 6); nothing was tuned on the real surfers' answers.

What the stress test found, and the fix. Before the fix, our formula missed every overhead day: it penalized boards that are too big for powerful surf but never boards that are too small, so it kept the regular shortboard instead of the step-up. We added two rules that only apply on big, powerful days: a board smaller or shorter than ideal loses catching ease and control (harder to paddle in early and to hold a line), and a step-up being up to 15% bigger than ideal is no longer penalized. The first version also applied the 15% allowance on small days, which broke three small/mid-day picks, so we limited it to big days before taking these numbers.

7. Limits and next steps

  • The formula is still being tuned. The persona stress test exposed a big-wave blind spot, now fixed (section 6 shows before and after). The remaining misses are small and punchy mid-size days, and the big-wave rule is sensitive to estimated liters when surfers only know their board's length.
  • Heuristics, not physics. The meters, sizing factors and safety limits are transparent formulas, not validated by shapers or lifeguards. The 3D view is illustrative.
  • Forecast is a model. Face height = significant wave height x a period factor, from a model grid point offshore; wind, tide and local bathymetry aren't included. NOAA buoy readings would add live ground truth for US spots.
  • Small evidence base. 4 real sessions from friends of the author; the ChatGPT comparison uses the same sessions.
  • Spot database. 54 curated spots; elsewhere, safety ratings use wave height only (no power or skill floor).

With another month (Tier 3 path): pilot with a South Bay surf shop. Jack's Surfboards is the natural first partner, since its owner suggested the shop feature. Staff and Quiver Match both recommend for real customers, and we measure agreement and whether staff keep using it. We'd also add NOAA buoys plus wind and tide, a "best hour to surf" pick, rental inventory, and a feedback button so every session improves the formula.
